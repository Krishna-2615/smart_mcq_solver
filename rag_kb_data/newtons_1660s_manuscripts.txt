# Newton's 1660s Manuscripts

Isaac Newton's manuscripts from the 1660s represent one of the most prolific, transformative periods of scientific creativity in history, establishing the empirical foundations of classical physics.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Calculus (Fluxions)**: The mathematical study of continuous change, developed independently by Newton to calculate rates of motion and areas under curves.
*   **Linear Inertia**: The physical principle stating that an object in motion will continue moving in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.
*   **Natural Philosophy**: The historical, pre-modern study of nature and the physical universe that preceded the establishment of formal empirical science.

## Core Concepts
Often referred to as his "annus mirabilis" or year of wonders, Newton retreated to his family estate at Woolsthorpe in the mid-1660s to escape the Great Plague of London. During this intense period of isolation, he drafted manuscripts that fundamentally revolutionized the understanding of nature. 

These documents trace his earliest developments of calculus, his pioneering optical experiments with prisms proving that white light is a composite of a full spectrum of colors, and his initial thoughts on universal gravitation. Crucially, the manuscripts reveal his evolving understanding of orbital mechanics; they document how Newton combined the concept of tangential, straight-line motion with the effects of a radially directed inward force to successfully express the mechanics of planetary orbits. These texts remain a primary historical record of the Scientific Revolution's birth.
