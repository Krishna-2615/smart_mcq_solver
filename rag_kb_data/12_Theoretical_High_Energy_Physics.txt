# Theoretical High Energy Physics

Theoretical high-energy physics explores mathematical frameworks that seek to unify the fundamental forces and explain the behavior of energy and matter under extreme conditions.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Supersymmetry**: A hypothesized spacetime symmetry that relates two basic classes of elementary particles: bosons, which carry forces, and fermions, which make up matter.
*   **Symmetry Breaking**: A phenomenon where a physical system that symmetric equations of motion govern ends up in an asymmetric state, either spontaneously or explicitly due to external terms.
*   **Inflaton**: The hypothetical scalar field responsible for driving the rapid, exponential metric expansion of space during the inflationary epoch of the early universe.

## Core Concepts
To address structural gaps in the Standard Model, physicists develop advanced mathematical principles. Supersymmetry posits that every known particle possesses a heavy partner particle with a spin value differing by a half-integer, providing an elegant pathway to balance quantum loops and unify interactions at extreme energies. 

When these symmetries are applied to stochastic systems governed by differential equations, they help maintain continuity across phase spaces via continuous time flows. Symmetries can be broken explicitly through defining mathematical terms that do not respect the symmetry, or spontaneously when the underlying physical actions remain perfectly symmetric but the absolute lowest-energy vacuum state violates that symmetry, yielding new localized particle states.
