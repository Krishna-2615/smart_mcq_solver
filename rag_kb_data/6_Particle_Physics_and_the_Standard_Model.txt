# Particle_Physics_and_the_Standard_Model

Particle physics is the branch of science that studies the fundamental constituents of matter and radiation, exploring the smallest observable building blocks of the universe.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Quark**: An elementary particle and a fundamental constituent of matter. Quarks combine to form composite particles called hadrons, the most stable of which are protons and neutrons.
*   **Gauge Boson**: A force-carrying particle that acts as a mediator for fundamental interactions between matter particles.
*   **Magnetic Monopole**: A hypothetical elementary particle that acts as an isolated magnet with only one magnetic pole, possessing either a north pole or a south pole exclusively.

## Core Concepts
The modern understanding of subatomic interactions is organized into the Standard Model of particle physics. This model catalogs all known elementary particles and describes three of the four fundamental forces: electromagnetism, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force. 

The strong interaction is governed by the principles of Quantum Chromodynamics, where particles possess color charge and are permanently bound together through color confinement, preventing individual quarks from ever being isolated in an independent state. Particles acquire their mass through continuous interactions with the pervasive Higgs field, mediated by the Higgs boson. The free neutron is inherently unstable outside an atomic nucleus, undergoing a specific decay process that releases an electron and an antineutrino, defining a precise threshold of decay energy.
