# Proximity-Focusing in RICH Detectors

Ring Imaging Cherenkov (RICH) detectors are vital instruments in high-energy physics, utilizing the proximity-focusing design to precisely identify fast-moving charged particles.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Cherenkov Radiation**: The electromagnetic radiation, typically visible as a faint blue glow, emitted when a charged particle passes through a dielectric medium at a speed greater than the phase velocity of light in that medium.
*   **RICH Detector**: A particle physics detector that determines the velocity of a charged particle by imaging the ring of Cherenkov light it emits.
*   **Radiator**: The specific transparent medium, such as an aerogel or a specialized gas, that the high-energy particle travels through to produce Cherenkov radiation.

## Core Concepts
When a particle accelerates through a radiator faster than light can travel through that same material, it emits a cone of Cherenkov light, conceptually similar to the sonic boom generated by an aircraft exceeding the speed of sound. The physical angle of this emitted light cone is directly and mathematically linked to the particle's exact velocity.

The proximity-focusing design is an elegant, compact architecture used in modern RICH detectors. In this setup, the thin radiator layer is placed a specific, short distance away from the photon detector plane. The emitted cone of Cherenkov light traverses this small gap and directly intersects the flat detector array. This intersection creates a highly distinct ring of light on the sensor. By analyzing the precise radius of this detected ring, physicists can definitively calculate the particle's velocity and, consequently, its identity.
