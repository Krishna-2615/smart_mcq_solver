# Classical Mechanics and Kinematics

Classical mechanics is the branch of physics that models the motion of macroscopic objects under the influence of forces. It is built upon the foundational laws formulated by Sir Isaac Newton, which describe how mass, force, and acceleration interact. 

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Mass**: An intrinsic, inertial property of an object that measures its resistance to any change in its state of motion.
*   **Force**: Any interaction that, when unopposed, will change the motion of an object. It can be understood as a push or a pull acting upon a mass.
*   **Acceleration**: The rate of change of velocity of an object with respect to time. It describes how quickly an object speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.
*   **Velocity**: The rate at which an object changes its position in a specific direction.

## Core Concepts
According to the laws of motion, an object will maintain a constant velocity unless acted upon by an external net force. When a net force is applied, the resulting acceleration is directly proportional to the magnitude of the force and inversely proportional to the mass of the object. 

Classical mechanics provides the framework for understanding macroscopic everyday movements, engineering architectures, and regular machinery. However, it operates within strict limits; it cannot accurately predict behavior when velocities approach the speed of light, nor can it model the probabilistic behavior of subatomic particles, where quantum phenomena dominate.
