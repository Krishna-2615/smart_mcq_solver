# Cell Biology and Anatomy

Cell biology and anatomy explore the structural frameworks, internal organ designs, and operational transport mechanisms that sustain living organisms.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Anatomy**: The branch of morphology that deals with the structural identification and internal organization of living organisms.
*   **Active Transport**: The energy-requiring movement of molecules or ions across a cellular membrane against a concentration gradient.
*   **Passive Transport**: The movement of substances across a cellular membrane along a concentration gradient, requiring zero metabolic energy input from the cell.

## Core Concepts
Living things are organized into hierarchical structural networks. Biological structures are described via morphology, which isolates and catalogs organs based on their specific functional contributions to the overall organism. 

At the microscopic scale, cells maintain structural integrity and internal balance by carefully regulating material movement across their membranes. Passive transport occurs naturally, driven entirely by the combined effects of concentration differences and electric fields. Active transport, conversely, forces molecules to move from low-density zones into high-density zones, a process that requires the cell to actively expend chemical energy. At the macroscopic scale, specialized anatomical features, like the fibrous cardiac skeleton, provide the essential structural support to separate heart chambers and anchor operational valve systems.
