# Genetics and Evolutionary_Biology

Genetics and evolutionary biology study how biological information is encoded, inherited, and systematically modified over successive generations, shaping the diversity of life.

## Fundamental Definitions
*   **Gene**: A distinct sequence of nucleotides forming part of a chromosome, acting as the fundamental unit of heredity that codes for specific functional proteins.
*   **Bilateral Symmetry**: A structural arrangement where an organism can be divided into identical matching halves along a single central longitudinal plane.
*   **Coevolution**: The process where two or more distinct species reciprocally influence each other's evolutionary pathways over long periods.

## Core Concepts
The development of structural form in organisms is heavily guided by genetic instructions. Specific transcription factors, coded by regulatory genes like the CYCLOIDEA family, actively manage complex developmental pathways, controlling how tissues expand to establish symmetrical physical layouts. 

Symmetry variations represent critical milestones in evolutionary history. When distinct species live in close proximity, they often develop deep ecological relationships that drive coevolutionary changes. This can result in mutualistic symbiosis, such as myrmecophyte plants evolving specialized structures to house and feed specific ant colonies in exchange for defense against herbivores. Similarly, soil fungi and plant roots coevolve mutually beneficial networks to optimize nutrient absorption, demonstrating how genetic adaptations shape broad ecological communities.
